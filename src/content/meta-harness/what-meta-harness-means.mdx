"Meta-harness" is a new term, and people use it for different things. Before building one, separate the meanings, and look at the research it grew from.

## Two senses of the word

1. **An outer system that optimizes a harness.** This is the sense in the March 2026 paper "Meta-Harness: End-to-End Optimization of Model Harnesses". The meta-harness searches over harness code, evaluates candidates, and learns from the results.
2. **A layer above several harnesses or agents.** Here the meta-harness orchestrates, routes or supervises multiple agents or tools. The word is also used as a product or project name in other places, which adds to the confusion.

This course concentrates on the first sense, because it is the better defined and best evidenced, and covers the second as an architecture pattern: a supervising layer that can choose, configure or improve the harnesses underneath it. When you read anything labeled "meta-harness", check which sense it uses.

## The research lineage

Automatic improvement of LLM systems has several earlier threads.

- **Prompt and program optimization.** *Large Language Models as Optimizers* (OPRO) uses an LLM to propose better prompts from past scores. *TextGrad* passes textual feedback "backwards" through a system. *DSPy* compiles declarative LM programs, tuning prompts and examples against a metric.
- **Self-reflection.** *Reflexion* lets an agent write verbal feedback about failures and use it on the next attempt. *Voyager* builds a growing library of skills as code.
- **Searching over agent designs.** *Automated Design of Agentic Systems (ADAS)* proposes a **meta agent** that writes new agent programs in code, guided by an archive of earlier designs. The authors report that discovered agents beat hand-designed ones on coding, science and math tasks and carried over to other domains and models.
- **Self-modifying agents.** The *Darwin Gödel Machine* keeps an archive of coding agents, uses a foundation model to produce variants of its own code, and validates each change empirically instead of trying to prove it. It reports SWE-bench performance rising from 20.0% to 50.0%, with safety measures such as sandboxing and human oversight.
- **Evolutionary code search.** *AlphaEvolve* pairs LLM-written code changes with automated evaluators to discover algorithms.

## What is new in Meta-Harness

The paper's argument is that many text optimizers throw away too much information: they compress feedback into a score or short summary before the optimizer sees it. Meta-Harness instead gives a coding-agent proposer **filesystem access to the full history** (source code, scores and execution traces of every earlier candidate) and lets it investigate with tools such as `grep` and `cat`, as an engineer debugging would. Lesson 5 covers the details and the evidence for this claim.

## A mental model

```text
          ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
          │ meta-harness (outer loop)              │
          │  proposer ── writes ──▶ harness code   │
          │     ▲                        │         │
          │  reads history          runs on tasks  │
          │     │                        ▼         │
          │  scores + traces ◀── evaluation        │
          └────────────────────────────────────────┘
                         │ selects
                         ▼
                  best harness(es) ──▶ your agent
```

The inner harness runs tasks with a **frozen** model. The outer loop changes only the harness. That separation is what makes results interpretable: gains come from better scaffolding, not new weights.

<Callout title="Keep a skeptic's eye">
  This is young research, mostly preprints with the authors' own benchmarks.
  Read results as promising evidence about a method, not as guarantees about
  your task. Lesson 6 shows how to test it on your own.
</Callout>
